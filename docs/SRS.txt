SRS – E-commerce Website for China Import Store (BD)
Nov 19, 2026 · @Showrov
Summary
This document specifies a single-brand e-commerce website for selling China-imported kitchen, home and lifestyle gadgets in Bangladesh. It has two parts: a storefront with a distinctive, motion-rich design, and an admin panel that controls everything, including products, orders, customers, and every section, banner, menu and colour of the storefront.
The store will launch without a payment gateway because there is no merchant account yet. Orders come in through a checkout form and are paid by Cash on Delivery (COD). Customers can optionally pay the delivery charge (or the full amount) in advance by bKash/Nagad Send Money and enter the transaction ID. Staff verify each order by phone or OTP and then confirm it. On confirmation the system creates an invoice and sends the customer an SMS and an email with a secure invoice link.
Customers can sign in with Google or with email/phone and a password. They can also check out as guests. The stack is Next.js (App Router) + TypeScript + MongoDB, with shadcn/ui for interface components, Framer Motion and GSAP for motion, and optional Three.js / React Three Fiber for 3D product views.
The system is designed for security first (OWASP-aligned controls, role-based admin access, audit logs, fraud checks on orders) and horizontal scalability (stateless app servers, CDN-served media, caching, background job queue).

1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines what the store website must do (functional requirements) and how well it must do it (non-functional requirements). It is the shared reference for the two founders, any developer or designer who joins, and later for testing and acceptance.
1.2 Scope
In scope for v1
	•	Public storefront: home, category/collection pages, search, product detail, cart, checkout, order tracking, account area, CMS pages (About, Policies, FAQ, Contact).
	•	Product media: multiple images and videos per product, variants (colour/size/model), stock per variant.
	•	Order capture without a payment gateway: COD, manual bKash/Nagad advance with transaction ID, zone-based delivery charge.
	•	Order verification and confirmation, invoice generation, SMS + email notifications.
	•	Customer authentication: Google sign-in, email/phone + password, guest checkout.
	•	Admin panel: catalogue, inventory, orders, customers, coupons, delivery zones, storefront builder (sections, banners, menus, theme), CMS pages, notification templates, staff and roles, reports, audit log, settings.
	•	Courier booking via API (Steadfast first).
Out of scope for v1 (planned later)
	•	Online payment gateway (bKash/Nagad merchant checkout, SSLCommerz, cards).
	•	Multi-vendor marketplace, wholesale/B2B portal.
	•	Native mobile apps (the site will be a mobile-first, installable PWA instead).
	•	Loyalty points, affiliate programme, live chat (a WhatsApp/Messenger button is in scope).

2. Receiving orders without a payment gateway
Short answer: yes, orders come in through a checkout form, but a structured one tied to the cart, stock and delivery zones, not a loose contact form. The customer pays by COD. For risky orders (or always, if you choose), they also pay the delivery charge in advance by bKash/Nagad Send Money and type in the TrxID. Staff check the payment and the customer and then press Confirm. That press triggers the invoice, the SMS and the email.

Receiving account. Avoid using a plain personal wallet for business receipts. bKash offers a Personal Retail Account (PRA) for micro and small retail and f-commerce sellers, and Bangladesh Bank allows these accounts without a trade licence (with e-KYC). Customers can pay a PRA by QR or by account number, which suits this manual flow. Check the current limits with bKash before launch. Why manual TrxID and not "Send Money" screenshots? A TrxID can be checked against the wallet's own transaction history in seconds. Screenshots are easy to fake.

2.3 How the delivery charge is calculated
	•	The customer picks a district and area. Each area belongs to one delivery zone set in admin (for example Inside Dhaka, Dhaka suburbs such as Savar, Gazipur and Narayanganj, and Outside Dhaka).
	•	Each zone has a base charge for the first kg and an extra charge per additional kg. The cart weight is the sum of variant weights (packaging weight is added from settings).
	•	Optional rules, all set in admin: free delivery above a cart value, free delivery on selected products, a flat-rate campaign, or a COD handling fee as a percentage.
	•	The server calculates the charge again at order placement. The client-side value is never trusted.
Example with admin-set values: Inside Dhaka ৳70 up to 1 kg + ৳15/kg; Outside Dhaka ৳130 up to 1 kg + ৳20/kg. Final values should match your courier contract.
2.4 Confirming the order (fake-order protection)
Fake and "try-and-refuse" orders are the main cost of COD. v1 uses layered checks:
	•	Phone OTP at checkout – a 6-digit code by SMS proves the number is real and owned by the person ordering. Codes expire in 5 minutes, allow at most 5 tries, and resends are rate-limited.
	•	Automatic risk score at placement, based on: a new vs returning phone, past cancellations/RTOs for this phone or address, order value, zone, multiple orders from the same IP/device in a short time, and a mismatch between name and past orders. High-risk orders require an advance payment, or are flagged for a call.
	•	Courier success-rate lookup (optional) – some couriers and third-party tools expose a phone's delivery success rate, which the panel shows beside the order.
	•	Staff verification in admin – staff see the order in a Pending queue, call the customer (click-to-call), check any TrxID against the wallet statement, then press Confirm or Cancel with a reason. Each action is recorded in the audit log.
	•	On Confirm – stock is committed, the invoice number is assigned, the invoice PDF is generated, and the SMS + email with the invoice link are queued.
2.5 What the customer receives
	•	Right after placing: an on-screen thank-you page with the order number and a short SMS such as "Order #SR10234 received. We will call you to confirm."
	•	On confirmation: SMS – "Order #SR10234 confirmed. Total ৳1,450 (COD ৳1,350). Invoice: https://yourshop.com/i/xxxxx" – and an email with the same link and a PDF attached.
	•	On shipping: SMS with the courier name and tracking code.
	•	On delivery / return: a status SMS and an optional review request.
SMS texts are kept short and stored as editable templates in admin (Bangla and English) so they fit one or two SMS segments.
3. Order lifecycle

order lifecycle · 8 states, 1 staff check
Every order starts as Pending with stock reserved. Nothing customer-facing beyond the "order received" SMS happens until a staff member verifies it. Only Confirmed triggers the invoice, the SMS and the email. Cancelled and returned orders give their stock back automatically. Allowed transitions are enforced on the server (FR-OR-01), and each change is written to the order's status history and the audit log.

The controls follow the OWASP Top 10 and the OWASP ASVS Level 2 as the target.

6. Architecture and tech stack
6.1 Overview
One Next.js (App Router) monorepo serves both the storefront and the admin panel, with a separate worker process for background jobs. Both talk to MongoDB and Redis. Media lives in object storage behind a CDN.
	•	Storefront (app/(store)) – mostly static/ISR pages, Server Components for data, client components only for interactive parts (cart, quick view, galleries, motion).
	•	Admin (app/admin) – dynamic, authenticated, never cached publicly; uses shadcn/ui data tables, forms and dialogs.
	•	API layer – Server Actions for form mutations from our own UI; Route Handlers (app/api/*) for webhooks (courier, SMS delivery reports), the invoice link, and any future mobile app.
	•	Domain modules (/modules/catalog, /orders, /cms, /auth, /notifications, /shipping, /inventory) hold business logic and validation; UI code never talks to the database directly.
	•	Worker – a Node.js process running BullMQ consumers for SMS, email, PDF invoices, courier booking, image/video processing and scheduled tasks (release expired stock reservations, publish scheduled banners).

6.3 Request flow for an order
	•	Browser submits the checkout Server Action with the cart ID, form data and an idempotency key.
	•	Server validates with Zod, checks the OTP session, recalculates prices, delivery charge and coupon, and computes the risk score.
	•	A MongoDB transaction creates the order, reserves stock, and writes an order.created event.
	•	The job queue sends the "order received" SMS and alerts staff (admin notification + optional Telegram/WhatsApp alert).
	•	Staff confirm in admin → transaction updates status, commits stock, assigns invoice number → jobs generate the PDF and send SMS + email.
	•	Staff (or an auto-rule) book the courier → courier consignment and tracking code saved → courier webhooks update status to Shipped / Delivered / Returned.

Design notes
	•	Order items keep a snapshot of title, price and cost, so later product edits never change past orders or profit reports.
	•	Order and invoice numbers come from the counters collection with an atomic increment, so they are sequential and never collide under load.
	•	Stock check and reservation use a conditional update (stock - reserved >= qty) inside the order transaction to prevent overselling.
	•	advance.trxId is unique so the same transaction ID cannot be reused on two orders.
	•	TTL indexes clean up OTPs, sessions and abandoned guest carts automatically.
8. Integrations
Every integration sits behind an adapter interface (e.g. SmsProvider.send(), CourierProvider.createParcel()), so a provider can be swapped or a fallback added from admin without touching business logic.
8.1 Google sign-in
	•	Create an OAuth client in Google Cloud Console (Web application). Authorised redirect URI: https://<domain>/api/auth/callback/google; separate clients for staging and production.
	•	Scopes: openid email profile only.
	•	The OAuth consent screen needs the app name, logo, support email, and links to the Privacy Policy and Terms pages (which the CMS provides).
8.2 SMS gateway (OTP and order messages)
	•	Business SMS in Bangladesh must go through an aggregator that BTRC lists as an authorised A2P SMS provider. International APIs such as Twilio are much more expensive per message and are not the right route.
	•	Two types: masking (your brand name as sender, e.g. "GADGETBD", needs sender-ID registration) and non-masking (numeric sender, cheaper). Use masking for order confirmations and invoices (trust), and non-masking or masking for OTP.
	•	Indicative prices from one local provider: about ৳0.40 per non-masking and ৳0.64 per masking SMS at the entry recharge, falling with larger recharges. BTRC also set a floor price for bulk SMS in 2025. Compare 2–3 providers before choosing.
	•	Requirements: HTTP/JSON API, delivery reports (webhook or polling), Unicode (Bangla) support, balance check API. Configure one primary and one fallback provider.
	•	Cost control: OTP only once per checkout session, short templates (one segment where possible), and email for long content.
8.3 Email
	•	Transactional provider (Resend, Amazon SES or Brevo) on a sending subdomain such as mail.<domain>, with SPF, DKIM and DMARC configured.
	•	Emails: verify email, reset password, order received, order confirmed (with invoice PDF attached + link), shipped, delivered, cancelled; staff invites and 2FA alerts.
	•	Templates built in React Email, editable subject/body text in admin.
8.4 Courier
	•	Steadfast first: its merchant API lets you create orders (single or bulk), get a consignment ID and tracking code, check status and check balance. API key and secret come from the merchant dashboard. Payload maps directly: invoice (our order number), recipient name, phone, address, COD amount, note.
	•	Pathao and RedX adapters next (both offer merchant APIs; Pathao needs city/zone/area IDs, so the locations collection stores each courier's IDs).
	•	Booking: one click per order or bulk; auto-book on confirm (optional per zone). Status updates via webhook where available, otherwise a polling job every 30–60 minutes.
	•	COD reconciliation: import courier payout reports (CSV or API) to mark orders as Paid and flag mismatches.
8.5 Payments (later phase)
	•	Once a trade licence and merchant account exist, add bKash Checkout / Tokenized, Nagad merchant and/or SSLCommerz behind a PaymentProvider adapter. The checkout already has a payment-method step, so this is an additional option, not a redesign.
	•	Until then, the manual advance flow (section 2) is used, with TrxID verification by staff.
8.6 Media storage and CDN
	•	Object storage (Cloudflare R2 / S3) or Cloudinary. Browser uploads directly via pre-signed URL; the worker creates image sizes (AVIF/WebP), video posters and compressed MP4/WebM.
	•	Public media cached on the CDN with long cache headers and versioned file names; invoice PDFs stored in a private bucket and served only through the signed invoice link.
8.7 Marketing and analytics
	•	Meta Pixel + Conversions API (server-side events for purchase/order confirmed, de-duplicated with the browser pixel), GA4, TikTok Pixel.
	•	Facebook/Instagram product catalogue feed (XML/CSV) generated from products for dynamic ads.
	•	Consent banner if required; analytics scripts loaded after the page is interactive.

9.1 Acceptance criteria (launch)
Ordering and confirmation
	•	A guest can place a COD order on a 360 px phone in under 2 minutes, including OTP.
	•	Delivery charge shown at checkout equals the zone rule for every test district (inside Dhaka, suburb, outside Dhaka) and changes with cart weight.
	•	Changing price or delivery charge in browser dev tools has no effect on the saved order.
	•	Submitting the same checkout twice (double click, network retry) creates exactly one order.
	•	When an outside-Dhaka order requires an advance, the order cannot be placed without a sender number and a TrxID; a TrxID used before is rejected.
	•	Two customers buying the last unit at the same moment: one succeeds, one sees "out of stock"; stock never goes negative.
	•	On Confirm, within 1 minute: invoice number assigned, PDF available, SMS and email delivered (or logged as failed with retry).
	•	The invoice link opens without login, shows correct totals and COD amount, and stops working after it is revoked or expired; changing one character of the token returns "not found".
Admin and content
	•	A manager can add a product with 6 images and 2 videos, set the hover image, and see it on the storefront within 1 minute without a redeploy.
	•	A manager can add, reorder, schedule and hide home-page sections and change theme colours, with a preview before publishing.
	•	A staff user cannot open settings, integrations, staff management or reports (403 from the server, not just a hidden menu).
	•	Every confirm, cancel, price change and setting change appears in the audit log with user, time and before/after values.
Security and performance
	•	Admin sign-in without the 2FA code fails; 6 wrong passwords lock the account for 15 minutes.
	•	Security headers score A on a header scanner; no critical/high vulnerabilities in dependency scan.
	•	OWASP ZAP baseline scan shows no high-risk findings.
	•	Home, category and product pages meet LCP ≤ 2.5 s and CLS ≤ 0.1 on mobile (Lighthouse, 4G throttling).
	•	Load test: 500 concurrent virtual users browsing and 20 orders/minute for 10 minutes with p95 under the targets in 5.3 and zero errors.
	•	A backup can be restored to staging and the site runs from it.
